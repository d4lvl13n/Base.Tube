## What makes a Rocket League thumbnail work

Rocket League clips are fast, and that is the problem. A goal that looks
incredible in motion turns into a small car and a small ball on a big green
field when it is frozen into a thumbnail. A good Rocket League thumbnail fixes
that. It zooms in on one shot, shows the movement with a boost trail and uses
the game's own contrast: blue against orange.

Many viewers see your thumbnail small, on a phone or in the sidebar. A wide
replay shot does not survive that size. A car in the air with the ball at its
nose does. Every rule on this page follows from that.

## One car, one ball, one shot

The car and the ball are the heroes. Get close: the car should fill a third
of the frame, the ball should be right next to it. Choose the angle that
shows the trick: upside down for a flip reset, low and diving for a save,
nose to nose for a 1v1. Fans know the car bodies, so an Octane, a Fennec, a
Dominus or a Breakout reads at a glance.

## Show the movement

A still image needs help to feel fast. A boost trail behind the car, a speed
streak behind the ball, sparks where the wheels touch: one of these is enough
to turn a frozen frame into a moment. For long plays like an air dribble, a
dotted line tracing the path tells the whole story.

## Blue against orange

The game already gives you the strongest colour contrast there is. Put the
blue team on one side and the orange team on the other, or light your car
blue against an orange goal. Use yellow for boost and the one detail that
must grab the eye, and keep purple for rank badges.

## Five layouts, one per video type

Most Rocket League videos fit one of five shapes: **freestyle**, **rank
climb**, **clutch save**, **face cam** or **versus**. The layout recipes above
give the structure of each one. Pick the layout that matches the promise of
your video, then change the car, the arena and the words.

## Text: short, italic, outlined

Three words is the limit. Use capitals, a heavy italic font and a thick black
outline: FLIP RESET, 0:00, BRONZE → SSL. The words should add something the
image cannot say on its own. To compare heavy display fonts, see the
[YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts).

## A Rocket League thumbnail background that stays behind

The Rocket League thumbnail background sets the mood and nothing else. A
bright daytime arena suits goals and guides. A dark night arena with stadium
lights suits a Rocket League montage thumbnail. Blur it so the car and the
ball stand out. More ideas in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Faces and reactions

Cars have no faces, so a face cam adds the emotion: your scream at an
overtime winner, your disbelief at an own goal. The examples on this page are
generated with Base.Tube and use the game's own cars and arenas; their face
cams show invented people.

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9. For file size and format limits, see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner empty, where YouTube shows the video length. Then put your thumbnail
next to other Rocket League videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester). No
tool can predict how many people will click before a video is live; when you
connect your channel, Base.Tube shows the real impressions and click-through
rate of each video.
