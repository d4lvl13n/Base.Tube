## Where the look comes from

"Cursed images" is an internet term for photos that feel wrong. Nothing in
them is obviously dangerous, but something is off: a chair where a chair
should not be, a room that looks lived in by nobody, a toy sitting like a
person. They are usually low-quality snapshots lit by harsh flash, with no
explanation. That missing explanation is the point.

A cursed thumbnail borrows the same feeling. Instead of shouting, it makes
the viewer stare. In a feed full of shocked faces and huge captions, a quiet,
strange photo stands out precisely because it does not look like a
thumbnail.

## What makes an image feel cursed

- **Ordinary setting.** Kitchens, corridors, laundromats, parking lots. The
  more normal the place, the stronger the wrong detail.
- **One wrong detail.** Wrong place, wrong size, wrong material, or a
  figure that is not quite a person.
- **Harsh flash.** Direct flash flattens everything, blows out highlights
  and creates hard black shadows. It feels like evidence, not art.
- **Snapshot framing.** Slightly crooked, a little careless, as if taken in
  a hurry.
- **No explanation.** No arrow, no caption telling you what to feel.

## How to make a cursed thumbnail

- **Start with the video's strangest real moment.** An odd find, a bizarre
  recipe, a strange room.
- **Shoot or generate it like a phone snapshot.** Direct flash, no
  cinematic grading.
- **Remove everything that looks like a normal thumbnail.** No big title,
  no reaction face, no glow.
- **Optional caption:** one tiny lower-case word.
- **Check readability.** The wrong detail must still be visible at phone
  size, and the image should not turn into a black rectangle.
- **Keep the bottom-right corner clear** for the video length.

## Which videos suit a cursed image thumbnail

The look fits more channels than horror. Cooking channels use it for
bizarre recipes. Property and travel channels use it for strange houses and
abandoned places. Story channels use it for late-night tales, and comedy
channels for odd finds and thrift-store hauls. The common thread is a real
oddity in the video that the thumbnail can show without explaining.

## Creepy, not gross

Unsettling works; disgusting does not. Gore, injuries and real tragedies
will get the video restricted and drive viewers away. An unsettling
thumbnail is about strangeness: a cake made of spaghetti, a mannequin at
dinner, a hotel with tiny doors. For other creepy-but-safe looks, see
[he ate them](/thumbnails/he-ate-them) and the
[3 AM thumbnail](/thumbnails/3am) page.
