## What the caption does

WHO IS HE? turns any image into a puzzle. The viewer sees a figure, cannot
place them, and reads a question that confirms the feeling. The only way to
get the answer is to watch. That is why the caption appears on so many
mystery, theory and gaming videos, and why people search for it to make
their own.

It belongs to a family of one-question captions that movie-theory channels
made famous. The best documented are [does he know](/thumbnails/does-he-know)
and [why is he lying](/thumbnails/why-is-he-lying), both from New Rockstars
thumbnails. We could not find a single documented origin for WHO IS HE?, so
this page focuses on how the format works rather than on one famous image.

## Three ways to build the mystery

### The silhouette

A hooded, backlit or masked figure with the face hidden. Fog, a glowing
doorway or a night rooftop do the work. It suits character reveals, new
team members, story mysteries and trailer breakdowns. Keep the outline of
the figure sharp so it reads at phone size even when the face is dark.

### Circled in the crowd

A wide scene, a stadium, a group photo, a game lobby, with one person
circled in red. The circle turns a normal crowd into a "spot the stranger"
game. It works for sports funny moments, event vlogs and old family photos.

### The newcomer

An unknown character next to familiar ones, with everyone else staring at
them. This is the gaming version: a mystery player in a lobby, a new fighter
in a trailer. The stares of the other characters point the viewer's eye.

## How to keep it readable

- One figure, one question. Two mysteries in one thumbnail cancel out.
- Strong contrast between the figure and the background.
- The caption in heavy condensed capitals, yellow or white, black outline.
- A who is this thumbnail on a photo can use a magnifying glass or a red
  circle; on a cinematic scene, light alone is enough.
- Leave the bottom-right corner clear for the video length.

## Mystery is not a trick

A good mystery thumbnail is a promise to answer. If the "stranger" turns out
to be nobody, viewers feel tricked. Don't blur a real stranger's face to make
a mystery, and don't hint that a celebrity appears. Original characters or
people who agreed to be in the video are the safe route.
