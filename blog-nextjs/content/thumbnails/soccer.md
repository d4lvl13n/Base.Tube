## What a soccer thumbnail has to show

Soccer is a wide game: twenty-two players on a big pitch. A soccer YouTube
thumbnail cannot show all of that. It has to zoom in on one instant: the boot
on the ball, the keeper's fingertips, the face after the final whistle. The
wide TV angle that works in the video turns into green with tiny dots at
thumbnail size.

Every example on this page uses invented players in invented kits, such as
teal and white, orange, purple or lime. Your thumbnail can feel like a big
match night without copying a club, a player or a league.

## Pick the moment of contact

Most soccer clips have one frame where everything happens at once. For a goal,
it is the moment the ball leaves the boot, or the moment it hits the net. For
a save, it is the keeper at full stretch. For a skill, it is the ball in the
middle of the trick. Pause there. A frame a second later, when the player is
already jogging back, has lost the story.

## Five layouts for soccer videos

- **Highlight**: one player at the peak of the action, floodlights and a
  blurred crowd.
- **Skill**: a low, close shot of the feet and the ball, in daylight.
- **Versus**: two players or two teams split by a VS badge, for previews,
  predictions and challenges.
- **Tactics**: the pitch from above with a few arrows and circles.
- **Pack opening**: glowing player cards for soccer video game channels.

Keep one layout per series. Viewers who watched your last preview should
recognise the next one before they read the title.

## Colour: let the grass work

The pitch gives you a big block of green for free. Push its saturation a
little, put a dark sky above it and use white and yellow for the text. Red is
for cards, misses and VS badges. Invent kit colours that stand out against
green: orange, purple, teal or white work better than dark green or brown.
The [YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds)
shows more ways to keep the player in front.

## Text: words, a score or a minute

One to three words in capitals: "WHAT A GOAL", "LEARN THIS", "WHO WINS".
Sometimes a number says more: a scoreline for a comeback, "90+4'" for a late
winner. Use a condensed sports font with a thick outline; the
[YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts) lists free fonts
that stay readable when small.

## Tactics and analysis

Analysis videos sell an idea, so show where to look. Use a clean top-down
pitch, darken what does not matter and draw at most three marks: an arrow for
the run, a circle for the space, a line for the press. A pitch covered in
lines looks like homework.

## Size and the final check

Export at 1280 × 720 pixels in 16:9; the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size) has the current
limits. Keep the bottom-right corner clear for the video length. Then see
your thumbnail among other videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare two
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester).
