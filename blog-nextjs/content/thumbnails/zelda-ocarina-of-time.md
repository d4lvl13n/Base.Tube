## What we know about the remake

Checked on 22 October 2026, on Nintendo's own pages:

- **Release date:** 5 November 2026. Nintendo announced the date on 8 September
  2026, during the Zelda 40th anniversary Direct.
- **Platform:** Nintendo Switch 2 only. The
  [US Nintendo store page](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-switch-2/)
  says "Exclusively for Nintendo Switch 2".
- **What changes:** Nintendo's
  [Australian game page](https://www.nintendo.com/au/games/nintendo-switch-2/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time/)
  lists reimagined visuals, a refreshed soundtrack, fully voiced cutscenes, a
  jump button, a dash, motion aiming, and the option to hum songs into the
  console's built-in microphone.
- **Players:** single player.

That list tells you which videos will exist: comparisons, first reactions to
the voiced scenes, tests of the new jump.

## Two audiences, one thumbnail each

An Ocarina of Time remake thumbnail talks to one of two people.

- **The returning player** knows the original by heart. What they want to see
  is the difference: what changed, what was cut, whether an old trick still
  works. Comparisons and secrets are made for them.
- **The first-time player** has never seen the game. They want the adventure
  itself: a huge world, a first dungeon, a first boss. Wonder sells better than
  detail.

An image aimed at both speaks to neither. Pick one: "GLOW UP" for the fan,
"DAY 1" for the newcomer.

## The comparison is the remake's signature thumbnail

Every big remake produces the same video first: old versus new.

- **Same scene, same angle.** If the camera moves between the halves, the
  viewer compares the framing instead of the graphics.
- **Make the old side look honestly old.** Low-poly shapes, flat textures and
  thick fog read as "1990s 3D" at phone size. A blurred image just looks
  broken.
- **Put the text over the calmer half,** usually the N64 side.

## Show the real game, never a real person

Fans click on what they recognise: Link, the Master Sword, the ocarina,
Ganondorf, Hyrule Field, the hearts on screen. The examples on this page are
generated with Base.Tube to show what an Ocarina of Time thumbnail can look
like, and they use the game's look and characters.

- **No real faces.** Every face cam in the examples is an invented face. Never
  generate a real creator, a Nintendo developer or a celebrity. On your
  channel, your own face works best.
- **Nothing that looks official.** The characters belong to Nintendo. Read its
  fan-content guidelines (linked above) and never present your video as made
  or approved by Nintendo.

## Words that fit an adventure

A heavy sans-serif reads best at phone size, in white or gold with a thick
black outline. Keep a carved-stone serif for one-word titles like "SOLVED". To
compare fonts, see the [YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts).

## Backgrounds: a real place, with one light

Use a place fans know, such as Kokiri Forest or the Temple of Time, and blur or
darken it so the subject stays sharp. More in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Plan for launch week

First reactions, "what's new" videos and N64 vs Switch 2 comparisons go up in
the first days after 5 November, so treat these Zelda thumbnail ideas as a
schedule: build those layouts now and add the words on launch day. Rankings,
guides and secrets come later, once players have finished the game or got
stuck.

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels in a 16:9 frame; the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size) has the current file
limits. Keep the bottom-right corner empty, where YouTube shows the video
length. Then put your thumbnail next to other videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester). No tool
can predict how many people will click before a video is live: the real test is
how it performs on YouTube.
