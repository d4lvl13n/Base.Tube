## What makes a FNAF thumbnail work

Five Nights at Freddy's is built on one feeling: something is in the dark and
it is getting closer. The best FNAF thumbnails put that feeling in one image.
Most of the frame is black. One animatronic is there, too close or barely
visible, and its eyes are the brightest thing on screen.

Viewers see your thumbnail small, on a phone or in a sidebar, next to other
horror videos. A dark frame with one bright point stands out. A bright, busy
frame full of characters looks like everything else and loses the fear.

## Darkness is the main colour

Start from black and add light only where it matters: a door light on Bonnie,
a monitor glow on the desk, a flashlight beam down the hall. Then pick one
accent colour that tells the story. Red means danger and jumpscares, sickly
green means Springtrap and night-vision cameras, neon pink and purple mean
the Mega Pizzaplex of Security Breach.

## One animatronic, one moment

Choose the animatronic the video is really about. Freddy Fazbear in the
doorway for a night run, Foxy in the West Hall for a jumpscare, Golden Freddy
or Springtrap for lore, Glamrock Freddy for Security Breach. Show it in one
clear moment: standing still and staring, lunging toward the camera, slumped
in a corner. The stillness is often scarier than the attack.

On face-cam thumbnails, the creator's face carries the fear. Make it large,
with one real reaction (a scream, a flinch, hands over the mouth), and put the
animatronic on the other side.

## Show the game's own interface

Fans recognise the series from its screens: the security office with its fan
and monitor, the camera feed with grain and static, the power meter, the
clock running from 12 AM to 6 AM. Even one of these tells the viewer which
game and which stakes. "1%" next to the power icon, or "5 AM" on the clock,
says more than a full sentence.

## Five layouts for five kinds of video

Most FNAF videos fit one of five shapes: **night**, **jumpscare**, **lore**,
**versus** or **then and now**. The layout recipes above give the structure
of each. Night runs and custom nights use the office. Reactions use the
jumpscare layout. Theories use one mysterious character and a question.
Rankings use a split versus frame, and series retrospectives put the old
pizzeria next to the Pizzaplex.

## Text: short and nervous

One to three words in capitals, white or red, with a dark outline: NOPE,
1% POWER, 6 AM, WHO IS HE?. A slightly distressed or pixel font can match the
game, but only if it stays readable at phone size. Compare options in the
[YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts).

## A FNAF thumbnail background that stays dark

The background is the office, a corridor or a camera feed, almost black,
with grain or static on top. Keep it darker than the animatronic. More ways
to build a background that stays behind the subject are in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9; for file limits see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner clear for the video length. Dark thumbnails can turn to mud on some
screens, so put yours next to other videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester).

No tool can predict how many people will click before a video is live. The
