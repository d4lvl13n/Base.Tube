## Speak the language of anime

Anime has its own visual grammar, and fans read it instantly. Speed lines mean
action. Glowing eyes mean power. A face half in shadow means a secret. A tear
on a smile means a goodbye. An anime YouTube thumbnail that uses this grammar
tells viewers the mood of the video before they read a word.

Every example on this page uses original characters we invented, drawn in an
anime style. No character, key visual or logo from an existing series appears
in them.

## One character, one emotion

Anime scenes often have many characters. A thumbnail cannot. Pick one, put
them large on one third of the frame, and give them one clear emotion:
determination, shock, sadness, a cold smile. Close-ups on the eyes work very
well because anime eyes are drawn to carry emotion.

## Five layouts for anime channels

- **Reaction**: the scene on most of the frame, your face small in a corner.
- **Theory**: a character half in shadow, a red circle on a detail, a
  question.
- **Tier list**: a ranking grid with one character breaking out of it.
- **Recommendation**: a calm, beautiful scene that sells the mood.
- **Edit**: a neon or glitch close-up for AMVs and edits, almost no text.

Keep one layout per series, so your theory videos and your reactions are easy
to tell apart.

## Colour moods

Choose the palette from the feeling of the video, not from the series:

- **Power and battles**: violet, cyan and white, with a glow.
- **Mystery and theories**: black, deep violet and one red accent.
- **Calm and romance**: pink, orange and soft blue skies.
- **Comedy**: flat bright colours and big outlined text.

## Text

One to three words in capitals: "EPISODE 12", "HE KNEW?", "HIDDEN GEM". Use a
heavy brush font or a bold sans-serif with a thick black outline; the
[YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts) lists free fonts that
stay readable when small. Japanese sound-effect shapes can decorate the
frame, but the words people need to read should be in their language.

## Spoilers

Anime viewers care about spoilers. A reaction thumbnail that shows the big
twist of a new episode can annoy the very people you want to reach. Show the
moment just before, a character's reaction, or a question instead.

## Size and the final check

Export at 1280 × 720 pixels in 16:9; the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size) has the current
limits. Keep the bottom-right corner clear for the video length. For anime
Shorts, see the [YouTube Shorts thumbnail ideas](/thumbnails/youtube-shorts).
Then check your thumbnail in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare two
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester).
