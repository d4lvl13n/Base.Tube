## What makes an Apex Legends thumbnail work

Apex Legends videos compete with every other battle royale on YouTube, and
with each other. The thumbnails that stand out show one legend, one moment
and one result, in a frame that says "Apex" before the viewer reads a word.

Many viewers see your thumbnail small: on a phone, in the sidebar, on a TV.
A full gameplay frame turns into noise at that size; enemies become dots and
the HUD becomes a blur. A big legend, a big number and a clear colour survive.
Every rule on this page follows from that.

## Let one number tell the result

Apex is a game of numbers: kills, damage, rank points, squads left. A huge
"20" or "4K" says more than any sentence. Make the number the biggest thing
in the frame after the legend, in red or gold with a thick black outline. If
the number is there, you often need no other text.

## Pick the right legend

Each legend has a silhouette fans recognise instantly: Wraith's portal,
Pathfinder's screen face, Bloodhound's mask, Gibraltar's dome shield. Show the
legend you played in the video, large, using their ability. One legend reads
better than a full squad. For tier lists and "best legend" videos, the legend
in question is the whole thumbnail.

## Five layouts, one per video type

Most Apex Legends videos fit one of five shapes:

- **Big number:** the result as a number next to the legend.
- **Rank climb:** a split frame from a low badge to a high one.
- **Legend close-up:** one legend huge, for guides and tier lists.
- **Face cam:** your reaction next to a clutch or a crazy shot.
- **Final ring:** a wide shot of the last circle and the squads left.

Pick the layout that matches the promise of your video. Keeping the same
layout across a series helps returning viewers spot your videos.

## Colours with a meaning

The game has its own colour code: red is Apex itself, purple and gold are the
best shields and loot, orange is the ring. Use red for your main word, gold
for numbers and loot, and orange for final-circle tension. Keep the rest of
the frame calm so the accent stands out.

## Text: numbers first, then words

Three words is the upper limit, and a number often replaces them. Use a heavy
condensed font with a thick black outline, like the game's sharp menus. See
the [YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts) for options.

## An Apex Legends thumbnail background

Use a real map: Kings Canyon, World's Edge, Olympus, Storm Point or Broken
Moon. Bright daylight for wins and guides, the orange ring and smoke for
endings. Blur it so it never fights the legend. More ideas in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9. For file size and format limits, see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner empty, where YouTube shows the video length. Then put your thumbnail
next to other Apex videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester). No tool
can predict how many people will click before a video is live. When you
connect your channel, Base.Tube shows the real impressions and click-through
rate of each video.
