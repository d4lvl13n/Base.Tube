## What makes a Valorant thumbnail work

Valorant videos fight for attention in a crowded feed: highlights, guides,
rank climbs, agent tier lists. The thumbnails that stand out do three things.
They show one play. They read in a second. And they use the colours players
already link to the game: a sharp red, a cool teal, a dark navy.

Many viewers see your thumbnail small, on a phone or in the sidebar. Crosshair
details and kill feed numbers vanish at that size. An agent's pose, a glowing
banner and a big word survive. Every rule on this page follows from that.

## One agent, one play

Valorant agents are easy to recognise by their silhouette and their colours:
Jett's white and blue, Reyna's purple glow, Raze's orange, Sage's teal. Pick
one, make them large, and show them doing the one thing the video is about:
the Operator shot, the defuse, the ultimate. The agent should look at the
target or the text, which leads the viewer's eye there.

## Show the interface players trust

A piece of the game's own interface tells fans what happened faster than any
words. The ace banner says five kills. A planted Spike with a timer says
clutch. A Radiant badge says rank. Use one of them, large and clean, and leave
the rest of the HUD out.

## Five layouts, one per video type

Most Valorant videos fit one of five shapes: **ace**, **rank climb**,
**clutch**, **face cam** or **versus**. The layout recipes above give the
structure of each one. Pick the layout that matches the promise of your video,
then change the agent, the map and the words. Keeping the same layout across a
series also helps returning viewers spot your videos.

## Text: short and sharp

Three words is the limit. Use capitals, a heavy, angular font and a thick
outline: ACE, 1V5, IRON → RADIANT. The words should add something the image
cannot say on its own. Never put crosshair codes or settings in the
thumbnail; they belong in the description. To compare heavy display fonts,
see the [YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts).

## A Valorant thumbnail background that stays behind

The Valorant thumbnail background sets the mood and nothing else. A bright,
painted map spot suits highlights and guides. A dark navy frame with a red or
teal glow suits montages and clutches. A Valorant montage thumbnail can be
almost empty: one agent, one glow, one name. Whatever you choose, blur or
darken it so the agent stands out. More ideas in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Faces and reactions

Agents keep a calm face, so a face cam adds the emotion: your shock at a
flick, your joy at a rank-up. Put your face large on one side and the moment
on the other. The examples on this page are generated with Base.Tube and use
the game's own agents and maps; their face cams show invented people.

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9. For file size and format limits, see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner empty, where YouTube shows the video length. Then put your thumbnail
next to other Valorant videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester). No
tool can predict how many people will click before a video is live; when you
connect your channel, Base.Tube shows the real impressions and click-through
rate of each video.
