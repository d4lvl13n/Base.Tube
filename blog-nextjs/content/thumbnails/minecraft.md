## The problem with Minecraft thumbnails

Minecraft videos are everywhere on YouTube, and almost every one of them
starts from the same picture: green grass blocks, brown dirt, a blue
sky, a cube-headed character. A viewer scrolling through Minecraft results sees
dozens of those side by side. The blocks tell them nothing new.

So the job of a Minecraft thumbnail is not to say "this is Minecraft". The
viewer already knows. Its job is to say what is different about this one
video: the castle that took 100 days, the boss fight with one heart left, the
new mob nobody has tamed yet.

## Show the change, not the game

Each kind of Minecraft video has its own way of showing change:

- **100 days:** put day one and day 100 side by side. A tiny hut next to a
  castle tells the whole story without a single word.
- **Versus:** put the two choices in a split frame, each with its own colour,
  so the viewer picks a side before clicking.
- **Boss fights:** make the boss huge and the player small, and show the
  stakes: a boss bar, the health bar almost empty.
- **Updates and secrets:** show the new mob or the hidden trick, bigger than
  it should be, with someone reacting to it.
- **Minecraft in real life:** show the surprise of the mix: the game on a
  strange device, a real landmark next to its block replica.

## A Minecraft thumbnail background that tells the story

The Minecraft background for a thumbnail sets the mood in one look, so choose
it before anything else:

- **Golden hour** (low, warm sunlight) makes builds look finished and
  valuable.
- **Night, a cave or the Deep Dark** says danger. Most of the frame can be
  dark navy, with one light on the subject.
- **The Nether or the End** says a boss fight and real risk.
- **An empty sky or void** says challenge: one block, nothing else.

Whatever you choose, blur it or use depth of field so the character and the
text stay sharp. Many creators also use shader packs or 3D renders for richer
light; a simple scene with good light usually beats a detailed scene with flat
light. More ideas in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Break the green

Grass green and dirt brown are the colours of every Minecraft video, so they
cannot be your accent. Pick one colour that stands out against them: diamond
cyan for a rare find, lava orange for danger, yellow for the main word, deep
navy for night. Use it on the one thing the viewer must see first.

## Faces made of cubes

A cube-shaped head cannot show much emotion. Let the pose do it: arms out in
shock, crouching to hide, looking up at something huge. A custom skin with big
eyes and an open mouth helps, and so does your own face if your channel shows
it: a face cam reacting next to the game adds the emotion the blocks cannot.
The examples on this page are generated with Base.Tube and use the game's look
and characters; their face cams show invented people, never real creators.

## Text: big, blocky, short

Pixel fonts match the game, but thin pixel letters blur when the thumbnail is
small. If you use one, make it very large and give it a thick black outline.
Otherwise, a heavy, square display font gives the same feel and reads better.
See the [YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts) for options.
Three words is the limit, and they should add something the image cannot say,
like "ONE HEART" or "DAY 100".

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9. For file size and format limits, see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner empty, where YouTube shows the video length. Then put your thumbnail
next to other Minecraft videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester). No tool
can tell you in advance how many people will click; that answer comes from
YouTube once the video is live.
