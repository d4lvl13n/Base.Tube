## What the meme means

WHY IS HE LYING? is an accusation dressed as a question. Next to a calm face,
it reads as a dry joke: the person looks completely serious, and the caption
calls them out anyway. Online, people post the image under any claim that
sounds false, from "I only spent ten minutes on this" to "I'm not even that
good at this game".

The image comes from New Rockstars, a channel that breaks down films and
series. Its thumbnail showed Wong from the Marvel films with the question in
heavy capitals. In 2022 people started using it as a reply on X, and it
joined the channel's other famous thumbnail memes, like
[does he know](/thumbnails/does-he-know). The whole family is covered on our
[New Rockstars-style page](/thumbnails/new-rockstars).

## Why it works in a thumbnail

A question creates curiosity; an accusation creates curiosity plus a little
conflict. The viewer wants to know what the lie is and whether it is really
a lie. That makes it a strong debunk thumbnail, and a hook for any video that tests claims: product reviews,
myth-busting, debunks, "I tried it for a week" videos.

It also stays light. Paired with a calm face or an innocent pet, the caption
signals humour, not drama.

## How to make one

- **Find the claim.** What does the video check? A battery life, a diet, a
  builder's promise, a dog's innocence.
- **Pick the face.** Calm, neutral, almost proud. A guilty or angry face
  makes it a different, heavier thumbnail.
- **Add the evidence.** A small detail that contradicts the claim, circled
  in red: the cable in the pocket, the pizza box under the table.
- **Write the question.** WHY IS HE LYING?, IS SHE LYING? or simply IS
  THIS TRUE? in heavy capitals with a black outline.
- **Light it cool.** Blue tones and hard side light feel like an
  interrogation without being dark.
- **Keep the bottom-right corner clear** for the video length.

## Accuse claims, not people

Thumbnails are seen by many more people than the video. Calling a real,
named person a liar in one is unfair if you are wrong, and it can be
legally risky. Point the question at a claim, a product, a fictional
character or a pet. If the video does show that someone misled people, let
the evidence in the video make the case.
