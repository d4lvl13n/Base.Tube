## What makes a Battlefield 6 thumbnail work

Battlefield 6 competes with every other shooter on YouTube, and many of those
thumbnails look the same: a soldier, a gun, a red arrow. The Battlefield 6
thumbnails that stand out show what the series is known for: large-scale war
and destruction. A building coming down, a tank pushing a flag, a helicopter
falling out of the sky.

Most viewers see your thumbnail small, on a phone or in a sidebar. Big shapes
survive that size: a collapsing tower, a tank, an explosion. Small details
like a weapon attachment or a scoreboard do not.

## Destruction is the hook

Destruction is the thing Battlefield does that other shooters do not, so put
it in the frame whenever the video has it. Show the moment of collapse, not
the rubble after it: the wall cracking, the dust exploding outward, soldiers
diving away. A rocket trail or a red arrow leading to the cause tells the
story in one look.

## One moment of war per image

Each image should promise one thing. A tank in the middle of the enemy team.
A helicopter brought down by a single rocket. A squad dragging a teammate to
cover while holding a flag. A sniper in a tower that is falling apart. When
you try to show all of them at once, the thumbnail turns into noise.

Soldiers wear helmets and masks, so their faces cannot carry the emotion.
Action does: diving, dragging, aiming. On face-cam thumbnails, your own face
adds the reaction the soldiers cannot show.

## Show the game's HUD

A piece of the real interface tells fans this is Battlefield 6 and not
another shooter: Conquest flag markers, green squad icons, red enemy markers,
the minimap, the class icons. It also sets the stakes. A flag marker labelled
B with enemies all around it says "hold the point" without any text.

## Five layouts for five kinds of video

Most Battlefield 6 videos fit one of five shapes: **destruction**, **face
cam**, **vehicle**, **then and now** or **squad**. The layout recipes above
give the structure of each. Pick the layout that matches the promise of your
video, then change the map, the colour and the words. Keeping the same layout
across a series helps returning viewers spot your videos.

## Text: three words, heavy and condensed

Use one to three words in heavy condensed capitals, white or orange, with a
thick black outline: BRING IT DOWN, 1 TANK, HOLD B. The words add what the
image cannot say on its own. To compare heavy display fonts, see the
[YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts).

## A Battlefield 6 thumbnail background

Use a real map with smoke and dust in the air. Fire and orange light suit big
fights, cold grey suits tense moments. The dust softens the background on its
own, so the subject in front stays sharp. More ideas in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9; for file limits see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner clear for the video length. Then put your thumbnail next to other
shooter videos in the [YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview)
and compare versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester).

No tool can predict how many people will click before a video is live. The
real answer comes from YouTube. When you connect your channel, Base.Tube shows
the real impressions and click-through rate of each video.
