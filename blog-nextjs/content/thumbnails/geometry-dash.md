## What makes a Geometry Dash thumbnail work

Geometry Dash is a game of tiny icons, tight timing and loud colours. That
makes it tricky on YouTube. A screenshot of a hard level looks amazing
full-screen, but at phone size the icon becomes a dot and the spikes become
noise. A good Geometry Dash thumbnail fixes that: it zooms in on one result
and lets the neon do the rest.

Fans read the game fast. They know the difficulty faces, the game modes and
the meaning of a frozen progress bar. Use those signs, large and clean, and
they will understand your video in a second.

## The result is the story

Every Geometry Dash video promises one result. Pick it and put it first:

- **A demon beaten:** the icon in front of the level, the Extreme Demon face
  behind it, the progress bar full.
- **A fail at 99%:** the icon shattering on the last spike, the number huge.
- **A level showcase:** a wide shot of the best-looking section, the icon
  inside it.
- **A reaction:** your face next to the moment the bar hits 100%.
- **A before and after:** an easy level next to a demon, a beginner next to a
  pro.

## Make the small things big

In the game, the icon is a small square and the difficulty face sits in a
menu. In the thumbnail, they should be the heroes. Zoom in on the icon until
its face is clear, or show the difficulty face as a big badge. A huge red
demon face tells the viewer "this is hard" before they read the title.

## Neon with intent

The game is full of colour, so a Geometry Dash thumbnail has to choose. Keep
the background dark and pick one neon accent: green for a win, red for a
demon or a death, cyan or magenta for hype and showcases. One bright colour
on a dark frame pops in the feed; six bright colours turn into mud.

## Text: a number or a word

Three words is the limit, and often one number is enough: 99%, 100%. Use a
heavy, rounded font with a thick black outline, close to the game's own
bubbly letters. Keep attempt counts and level IDs in the title, not the image.
To compare heavy display fonts, see the
[YouTube thumbnail fonts guide](/youtube-thumbnail-fonts).

## A Geometry Dash thumbnail background that stays behind

The Geometry Dash thumbnail background is usually the level itself, darkened
and blurred behind the icon. For a showcase, the level can be the subject,
but one shape should still lead the eye: a portal, a dragon decoration, a
wave corridor. More ideas in the
[YouTube thumbnail backgrounds guide](/youtube-thumbnail-backgrounds).

## Faces and reactions

The cube has one face, so a face cam adds the emotion: the scream at 99%, the
tears at 100%. Put your face large on one side and the level on the other.
The examples on this page are generated with Base.Tube and use the game's own
icons, levels and difficulty faces; their face cams show invented people.

## Size and checks

Upload at 1280 × 720 pixels, in 16:9. For file size and format limits, see the
[YouTube thumbnail size guide](/youtube-thumbnail-size). Keep the bottom-right
corner empty, where YouTube shows the video length. Then put your thumbnail
next to other Geometry Dash videos in the
[YouTube thumbnail preview](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview) and compare
versions with the [thumbnail tester](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester). No
tool can predict how many people will click before a video is live; when you
connect your channel, Base.Tube shows the real impressions and click-through
rate of each video.
